Project Overview

A comprehensive masterplan for a sustainable, affordable mixed-income neighbourhood in the Nairobi Metropolitan Area — responding directly to the city's acute housing crisis.

At the unprecedented rate at which informal settlements are growing across the Nairobi Metropolis, it is clear there is a housing crisis. In line with the UN Sustainable Development Goals and Kenya's Constitutional bill of rights, every citizen is entitled to dignified housing. The current housing supply rate is far below demand — this project proposes a replicable, scalable design intervention.

The design is organised around a superblock / cluster model. Each cluster surrounds a shared courtyard providing communal interaction space, children's play areas, and vegetative cooling. Clusters are positioned and shaped to maximise views, daylight, and natural ventilation for every unit.

Housing Typologies

The scheme offers a full range of unit types across five income brackets, allowing mixed-income communities to form naturally within the same block:

  • Low Income: Studio, one-bedroom, and two-bedroom units (30–58m²) with modular layouts that can adapt as family size grows. Each includes a bicycle parking norch.
  • Lower Middle Income: Two and three-bedroom apartments on upper floors, all bathrooms ensuite, with open social spaces between units at each level.
  • Upper Middle Income: Four-bedroom duplexes spanning two levels, with double-height lounge, waterfall-island kitchen, servants' quarters, and extensive private terraces that bring the mansion-ette experience to a multi-level block.

Livelihoods & Ground Floor Activation

The ground floor is entirely commercial, with four typologies ranging from small regular shops to stay-in live-work units and large commercial stores suitable for a supermarket or hypermarket. This integrates livelihoods generation into the fabric of the neighbourhood from day one.

Mobility & Sustainability

The scheme promotes non-motorised transport with colour-coded, segregated bicycle and walking lanes separated from vehicular routes by planters. Vehicular basement parking is accessed from a secondary street and clearly visible from the public realm. Vegetative cooling, carbon absorption, and shading are integrated throughout the landscape strategy.

Design Response

  • Cluster-based superblock organisation
  • 5 income bracket housing typologies
  • Modular & adaptable unit design
  • Ground-floor commercial livelihoods
  • Pedestrianised & cycling-first streets
  • Communal courtyards & social spaces
  • Laundry rooms per floor (no balcony drying)
  • Universal access & wheelchair-friendly circulation
  • Aligned with UN SDGs & Kenya Constitution

Context

This project is part of our academic portfolio and represents our research position on affordable housing in Kenya. It directly informs our approach to residential and urban consultancy commissions.
